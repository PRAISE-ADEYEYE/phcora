The problem

Shortages stay invisible until a patient arrives.

Clinics report stock to the health system late, partly, or not at all. A supply officer cannot tell which clinics are running low and which are only silent.

34.3%of PHCs had essential medicines in stock and unexpired on survey day.

National Health Facility Survey 2023

39%of surveyed Osun PHCs had medications, against 80% in Anambra.

CheckMyPHC state surveys

About halfof surveyed PHCs in Benue and Anambra gave no answer on how often they are restocked.

The solution

Two questions decide every clinic.

How likely is it to run out before stock can arrive, and how much can we trust what it reports? The answers become one clear action.

Resupply

High risk, reliable reports. Send stock.

Verify then resupply

High risk, weak reports. Call to confirm and queue stock in parallel.

Verify

Looks fine, but the reports are too weak to trust. A phone call is the cheapest check.

Watch

Low risk, reliable reports. Leave alone.

How it works

Detect, assess, explain, prioritise, act.

Detect

Find clinics whose stock is falling or whose reports are going quiet.

Assess

Estimate the chance of running out before a resupply can arrive, with a range.

Explain

Show the reasons, and label every number as real, derived, predicted or simulated.

Prioritise

Rank clinics against the visits and stock the state can actually deliver.

Act

Choose resupply, verify or watch for each clinic.

Honest data

We never pass simulated numbers off as real.

Nigeria has no public facility level stock history. PHCORA uses real clinic locations and boundaries, calibrates to real state surveys, and simulates the monthly stock histories. Every number says which kind it is.
